# 5 Things I've Learned About How AI Search Picks Its Citations

> These are real patterns I've observed analyzing brand visibility in AI-generated answers.
> Not theory — just what the data keeps telling me.

---

**1. Being first doesn't mean being cited.**

Traditional SEO rewards ranking #1. AI search doesn't care. I've seen pages ranking position 8–12 get cited consistently while the top result gets ignored. What matters more: how clearly and directly the page answers the question.

---

**2. Structure beats depth.**

Long, comprehensive articles often lose to shorter, well-structured ones. If an AI can't parse your answer in 2–3 sentences, it moves on. Headers, clear definitions, and direct statements win.

---

**3. Brand mentions ≠ brand citations.**

A brand can be mentioned hundreds of times across the web and still get zero citations in AI answers. What gets cited is the *source* — not just the subject. If you're not publishing content, you're invisible.

---

**4. Some domains have a "trust tax."**

Certain domains get cited at a much higher rate than their traffic or authority would predict. Reddit, Wikipedia, and a handful of niche publishers punch way above their weight. The pattern suggests AI models have baked-in source preferences that don't fully align with traditional authority signals.

---

**5. Citation share is becoming a real metric.**

Impressions, clicks, rankings — these are the old scoreboard. The new one is: *when someone asks an AI about your category, does your brand show up in the answer?* That's citation share. And right now, most brands aren't tracking it at all.
